# Neural network reference

The neural close-up uses original procedural 3D particle geometry after the user-supplied photograph of neurons: one large cell in focus, four neighbours receding into soft focus, thick translucent silver-violet dendrites and warm amber light that ignites along them as the impulse travels. It is an artistic visualization, not a medically exact anatomical model.

The architectural finale uses the user-supplied `blueprient-to-house.mp4` (10.04 seconds). The white particle blueprint is sampled from the first video frame (32,768 points in `app/blueprint-points.json`) and assembled progressively, then crossfades to the supplied video, whose frame is controlled by scroll in both directions. `public/video/blueprint-to-house.mp4` is a muted 1280×720 H.264 derivative (~5.4 MB, frequent keyframes) for local playback. The completed white drawing remains visible as the loading/error fallback. Its 16:9 plane matches the video rectangle on desktop and mobile; playback begins only after the initial crossfade. This replaces the earlier Casa Marea website-layout study.

The current sequence is the GM (hero), the progressively drawn blueprint and architectural video (method), then the projects, services and idea scene before the form. All transitions reverse with scroll. Hover affects the GM, drawing and brain; the GM and brain can also be turned by dragging. The site-wide ambient starfield remains, without a nebula or blue halo. The earlier neural close-up is no longer part of the site.

The brain returns to the deterministic anatomical object's geometry. `assets/brain-source.bin` and its lossless JSON archive remain unchanged. The displayed `public/brain-sculpture.bin` is prepared offline by `scripts/build-brain-sculpture.py`: short local triangles connect original samples only when their normals and tangent directions agree; area sampling and progressive minimum-distance selection fill sparse regions and reject clusters. All generated positions are barycentric interpolations of original samples, without silhouette inflation, image-based displacement or camera-dependent geometry. Original bounds are preserved. The display is a local surface reconstruction, not a bit-for-bit copy of the original sample positions. The 98,304-star mobile prefix and complete 196,608-star desktop cloud cover the whole anatomy. The generator requires NumPy and SciPy; browsers only decode a precomputed asset.

The desktop material follows the user-selected [live reference](https://astra-local-c0ox.vercel.app/#pensiero) on the unchanged anatomical skeleton. `scripts/build-brain-surface.py` bakes its front-image tone and warped 3D ridge tones into `public/brain-surface.bin`; there are no positions or normals in this material asset. The displayed 163,840 points have the reference's five material roles: front, rim, far, and two fill layers. The particle palette keeps the white, blue, and warm selection; size is `7.5 + shade × 6.5`, and base light is `(0.035 + shade² × 1.6) × 0.78`, followed by broad anatomical-normal lighting, depth cue, and the Gaussian kernel (850/200). Secondary cerebral furrows receive a slight contrast reduction, while the central region and cerebellum retain their dark grooves. Far and soft-fill roles are dimmed so they do not suggest an exposed inner volume. A dark depth pass blocks the sky and cells behind the front surface while allowing adjacent cells of the same gyrus to remain lit. Anatomical normals reject back-facing points. There is no hemisphere opacity crossfade during rotation. The anatomical source and display binary remain unchanged. Mobile rendering and page behavior remain as before this surface-only iteration.

The prior image-shaped sculpture experiment was removed following the user's request to return to the anatomical geometry. The photograph remains an aesthetic reference; it does not define the current geometry.

The earlier two-terminal study used Alfred Pasieka / Science Photo Library's [Synapse illustration](https://sciencephotogallery.com/featured/2-synapse-illustration-alfred-pasiekascience-photo-library.html) as a composition reference. That terminal composition is no longer used in the current scene.
