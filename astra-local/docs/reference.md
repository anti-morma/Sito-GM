# Neural network reference

The neural close-up uses original procedural 3D particle geometry after the user-supplied photograph of neurons: one large cell in focus, four neighbours receding into soft focus, thick translucent silver-violet dendrites and warm amber light that ignites along them as the impulse travels. It is an artistic visualization, not a medically exact anatomical model.

The architectural finale uses the user-supplied `blueprient-to-house.mp4` (10.04 seconds). The white particle blueprint is sampled from the first video frame (32,768 points in `app/blueprint-points.json`) and assembled progressively, then crossfades to the supplied video, whose frame is controlled by scroll in both directions. `public/video/blueprint-to-house.mp4` is a muted 1280×720 H.264 derivative (~5.4 MB, frequent keyframes) for local playback. The completed white drawing remains visible as the loading/error fallback. Its 16:9 plane matches the video rectangle on desktop and mobile; playback begins only after the initial crossfade. This replaces the earlier Casa Marea website-layout study.

The current sequence is the GM (hero), the progressively drawn blueprint and the architectural video (method), then the projects, services and idea scene before the form. The brain uses the original 65,536 volumetric samples, converted losslessly from `assets/brain-source.bin` to `public/brain-model.json`. Its appearance channel uses the previous reference image while leaving every position and normal unchanged; no image points or procedural substitute create the geometry. `scripts/convert-brain.py` works offline and verifies an exact binary round trip. Source attribution stays in the model metadata. All transitions reverse with scroll. Hover affects the GM, drawing and brain; the GM and brain can also be turned by dragging. The site-wide ambient starfield remains behind the scenes. The earlier neural close-up is no longer part of the site.

`app/brain-density.ts` fills small local triangles between samples with matching surface normals and nearly tangent edges. Four deterministic candidates are compared to fill gaps instead of producing lines or clusters. The desktop scene supports 196,608 samples; phones support 65,536. Original samples remain unchanged; duplicate additions are suppressed, and local density controls each star's light contribution. Preparation yields periodically to the page and respects cancellation.

The current material follows the user-selected [live reference](https://astra-local-c0ox.vercel.app/#pensiero): fine white/blue stars, the same compact Gaussian particle kernel (850/200), point-size range (7.5 + shade × 6.5) and original star palette. The reference's tonal distribution is transferred onto the deterministic volume: nearby geometry above each tangent plane darkens real cavities, with local smoothing that respects opposing folds. This object-space material replaces the earlier planar image projection. An additive grazing-light term highlights the actual gyri without fading the underlying surface. Local density compensation limits bright clumps.

The brain uses a depth-only pass over the visible star cores to hide overlapping rear samples. Rotation does not fade points according to their facing angle or hemisphere, and the assembled surface has no particle drift. The small occlusion footprint preserves neighbouring fine stars. Entry and exit transitions still follow scrolling. The depth pass is enabled only in the brain scene and shares its geometry and uniforms with the colour pass.

The earlier two-terminal study used Alfred Pasieka / Science Photo Library's [Synapse illustration](https://sciencephotogallery.com/featured/2-synapse-illustration-alfred-pasiekascience-photo-library.html) as a composition reference. That terminal composition is no longer used in the current scene.
